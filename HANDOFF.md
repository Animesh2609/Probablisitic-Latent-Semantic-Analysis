# Group 12 — Probabilistic Latent Semantic Analysis for Information Retrieval

An experimental information retrieval project exploring **Probabilistic Latent Semantic Analysis (PLSA)** and comparing topic-based document retrieval against **TF-IDF / Vector Space Model (VSM)** and **Latent Semantic Indexing (LSI)** on the **20 Newsgroups** dataset.

> **Project status:** The repository experiments include the 20-category baseline and an improved 20-to-6-supercategory notebook. Both have been updated to calculate **Precision@10, Recall@10, MAP@10 and NDCG@10**. The improved notebook additionally implements validation-based model selection and a separately labeled PLSA + TF-IDF hybrid. Full experiment results must be generated by running the notebooks; no unverified performance claims are made here.

## 1. Objectives

- Implement PLSA from first principles using the Expectation–Maximization (EM) algorithm.
- Explore latent topic distributions and document-topic mixtures.
- Study whether topic representations support information retrieval beyond direct keyword matching.
- Compare PLSA retrieval with TF-IDF/VSM and LSI under a common evaluation setup.
- Investigate sensitivity to the number of latent topics and EM initialization.
- Analyze controlled mixtures of documents from different subject categories.

## 2. Dataset

The baseline uses the **20 Newsgroups** dataset through `sklearn.datasets.fetch_20newsgroups(subset="all")`.

- **Corpus:** all 20 original newsgroup categories, rather than a six-category subset.
- **Text cleanup:** dataset headers, footers and quoted passages are removed during loading.
- **Preprocessing:** spaCy-based normalization/lemmatization and stop-word filtering.
- **Features:** a training-fitted `CountVectorizer` with up to 3,000 vocabulary terms.
- **Data split:** stratified training/test split; vocabulary and retrieval models are fitted on training documents only.

Ground-truth newsgroup labels are used for stratification, relevance proxies and the controlled mixture experiment. **PLSA itself is trained without class labels.**

## 3. Methods

### PLSA

PLSA models the probability of a word occurring in a document as a mixture of latent topics:

\[
P(w\mid d)=\sum_{z=1}^{K} P(w\mid z)P(z\mid d)
\]

where `K` is the number of latent topics, `P(w|z)` is the topic–word distribution, and `P(z|d)` is the document–topic distribution.

The notebook implements the EM algorithm using NumPy and sparse document-term counts. It supports multiple random initializations, tracks training log-likelihood, inspects high-probability topic words and folds unseen documents/queries into the trained topic space.

### Retrieval baselines

- **TF-IDF/VSM:** lexical term-weighting and cosine-similarity retrieval.
- **LSI:** truncated SVD of the TF-IDF representation, followed by similarity-based retrieval.
- **PLSA baseline:** topic-space representation and similarity-based retrieval.

The baseline evaluates the same held-out documents and category-derived query relevance labels for each method.

### Controlled topic-mixture experiment

The notebook constructs synthetic documents by mixing words from baseball and politics-related source documents. It examines how the inferred PLSA topic mixture changes with the known source-word ratio. This is a diagnostic of learned topic behavior, **not** a guarantee of exact mixture-proportion recovery.

### Model-order and tempered-EM exploration

The baseline studies topic counts `K = 2, 3, 5, 8, 10` and includes an exploratory tempered E-step experiment using several temperature (`beta`) settings. These analyses are exploratory and should not be interpreted as validated retrieval hyperparameter selection.

## 4. Evaluation metrics

The updated notebooks use **four primary retrieval metrics**, calculated at **k = 10** for **PLSA, TF-IDF and LSI**. A retrieved document is treated as relevant when its category matches the query's target category: one of the original 20 categories in the baseline, or one of the six merged supercategories in the 20-to-6 experiment.

| Metric | Definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Precision@10** | Relevant documents in top 10 / 10 | How many retrieved results are relevant? |
| **Recall@10** | Relevant documents in top 10 / all relevant documents in the evaluated collection | What fraction of the available relevant documents did we retrieve? |
| **MAP@10** | Mean of AP@10 across queries | Rewards placing relevant results early across queries |
| **NDCG@10** | DCG@10 / ideal DCG@10 | Measures ranking quality with logarithmic discounts for lower positions |

### Metric formulas

For a query, let `rel_i` be **1** if the document at rank `i` belongs to the query's relevant category, otherwise **0**. Let `R` be the number of relevant documents in the evaluated collection.

**Precision and recall:**

```text
Precision@10 = (sum of rel_i for ranks 1..10) / 10
Recall@10    = (sum of rel_i for ranks 1..10) / R
```

**Average Precision and MAP:**

```text
AP@10  = sum(Precision@i * rel_i for i = 1..10) / min(R, 10)
MAP@10 = mean(AP@10 across all queries)
```

**Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain:**

```text
DCG@10  = sum(rel_i / log2(i + 1) for i = 1..10)
IDCG@10 = ideal DCG@10 for min(R, 10) relevant documents
NDCG@10 = DCG@10 / IDCG@10
```

For queries with no relevant documents, metrics should be handled explicitly; the notebooks' normal evaluation assumes each target category is represented in the test collection.

### Model selection in the improved experiment

The improved notebook uses a **60% training / 20% validation / 20% held-out test** split. It evaluates multiple PLSA topic counts and retrieval scoring strategies. **Validation MAP@10** is the primary selection criterion, followed by **validation NDCG@10** and then **validation Precision@10** for ties. The selected configuration is evaluated once on the held-out test set, reporting **Precision@10, Recall@10, MAP@10 and NDCG@10**; MRR@10 is also retained as a supplementary metric in Section 15.

The improved notebook reports **pure PLSA** separately from the **PLSA + TF-IDF hybrid**. Hybrid performance must not be presented as pure PLSA performance.

## 7. Experiment variants and extensions

- **20-category baseline:** uses all original Newsgroups categories, topic-mixture analysis, and PLSA vs. TF-IDF/LSI retrieval comparisons; reports the four primary metrics.
- **20-to-6 supercategory experiment:** retains documents from all 20 original categories but maps their relevance labels into six broad groups. This is **manual label aggregation**, not automatic discovery of six classes by PLSA.
- **Improved retrieval (Section 15):** compares PLSA cosine scoring and PLSA query-likelihood scoring; tunes topic count and scoring on validation data; compares against TF-IDF, LSI and an explicitly labeled PLSA + TF-IDF hybrid on held-out test data. The additional experiment uses 18 queries (three per merged supercategory).
- **Future extensions:** runtime/memory profiling, reusable `src/` modules and additional relevance judgments may be added as separately verified work.

For fair comparisons, methods within an experiment must use the same corpus split, query set, candidate pool and relevance definition. Results from the original 20-label experiment and the merged six-label experiment are **not directly interchangeable**.

## 9. Limitations

- PLSA maximizes data likelihood; it does not directly optimize retrieval metrics.
- Learned topic IDs do not necessarily correspond to human newsgroup labels.
- Topic count, initialization, preprocessing and query wording affect performance.
- TF-IDF can be stronger for exact lexical matches; LSI can also be competitive.
- Category membership is only a proxy for document relevance.
- The baseline does not use a separate validation split to select retrieval hyperparameters; Section 15 of the improved notebook does.
- Improvements should be demonstrated through held-out measurements rather than assumed.
